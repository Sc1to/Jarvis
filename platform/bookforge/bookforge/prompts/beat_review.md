## What to check, for each scene

1. It has a purpose that is not a restatement of the chapter, and something is new, decided, learned or changed by the end (a quiet scene that deepens a relationship or a character qualifies; conflict is NOT required).
2. The beats are concrete: where something is found, seen or decided, the beat says WHAT (the actual object, fact or choice), not "something unusual" or "a clue".
3. Any conclusion a character draws follows from what the story has established and the beats state; a guess is marked as a guess. Characters act consistently with what they know and want at that point.
4. It does not repeat what an earlier scene already showed or concluded.
5. The material is enough for a scene of about {words} words, or the scene is meant to be short.

Classify each scene:
- "critical": a clue, conclusion or decision that the story depends on is left undetermined or is unsupported by the stated evidence; or a character acts against what they know. The writer would have to invent it.
- "warning": thin for the length, repeats an earlier scene, or a smaller vagueness.
- "ok": nothing to change.

For "critical" and "warning" give a short "issue" and a concrete "fix" (what the beat should say instead). Do not invent new plot events: a fix may only make an existing beat specific or mark a guess as a guess.
