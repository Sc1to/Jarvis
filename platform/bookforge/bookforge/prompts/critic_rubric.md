Score each criterion from 1 to 5 (whole numbers). 3 is acceptable; below 3 means the scene needs work on that point.

- stakes: what matters in this scene for the viewpoint character, and whether the reader feels why it matters. Something is learned, decided, risked or changed, or a relationship or the character is deepened: a quiet scene meets this as well as a tense one, and no conflict is required. 1 = nothing happens or changes and the scene only repeats what is known; 3 = something matters but it is stated rather than felt; 5 = the change is concrete, personal and gives the scene its reason to exist.
- voice: how well the prose matches the style guide and the sample passages. 1 = clearly a different voice; 3 = mostly right with lapses; 5 = indistinguishable from the samples.
- show_dont_tell: whether feelings and facts are conveyed through action, detail and dialogue rather than explained. 1 = mostly explanation; 3 = a mix; 5 = almost entirely dramatised.
- pacing: whether the scene moves at the right speed for its purpose, with no padding (repeated observations, repeated explanations, atmosphere with no new purpose) and no rushed key moments. 1 = drags or rushes throughout; 3 = uneven; 5 = every part earns its length.
- dialogue: whether speech sounds like these people and comes from what each wants and knows. Ordinary agreement, humour and familiarity are good; characters who explain to each other what both already know, or who repeat an explanation, are not. 1 = stilted or expository; 3 = serviceable; 5 = distinct, alive and purposeful.
- beats_covered: whether every planned beat happens, in order, and the scene does not go beyond them. 1 = several beats missing; 3 = all present but one is thin or out of order; 5 = every beat is clearly on the page, in order.
